# The method

Five phases to capture, map and govern domain knowledge. The same method works on the internal knowledge of a company; here it runs on public sources only.

## 1. Scoping

Agree on the perimeter and write 3–5 competency questions: the questions the knowledge graph must answer. They drive the ontology, the tests and the scope. Measure the starting point: how long it takes today to answer them, and from which sources.

## 2. Processes and roles

Map the processes in scope and the roles involved. For each piece of knowledge, a RACI: who produces it, who owns it, who validates it, who uses it. In the public version, processes come from public market rules and timelines.

## 3. Knowledge mapping

Inventory the sources, explicit and tacit. Classify them, assess their quality and criticality, and spot knowledge that depends on a single person or a single document.

## 4. Modelling and prototype

Design a minimal ontology on open standards, guided by the competency questions. Populate the knowledge graph with LLM-assisted extraction and human validation. Query it directly and through an agent.

## 5. Validation and scaling

Test the graph on the competency questions and compare the results with the starting point. Define owners and an update process, so the graph stays alive after the project.

## Measures

- Share of competency questions answered correctly
- Time to answer an operational question
- Estimated onboarding time
- Number of processes that depend on a single expert
- Share of processes with a knowledge owner
